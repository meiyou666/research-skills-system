# Research Layers

Use the earliest unresolved layer as the current working level.

| Layer | Required decision | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence | What is supported within the audited search? | source ledger and evidence matrix |
| Gap | Which connection, boundary, or conflict remains unresolved? | bounded gap statement |
| Research object | What unit, population, context, and outcome are studied? | frozen scope |
| Question | Which unknown proposition is answerable? | dependency-ordered questions |
| Hypothesis | What conditional relationship is expected and falsifiable? | hypothesis cards |
| Candidate mechanism | What intervention or explanation may test the hypothesis? | candidate list for downstream design |
| Experiment | How will the hypothesis be tested? | downstream protocol |

## Diagnose a level jump

For a proposed implementation or optimization, ask:

1. Which frozen question does it answer?
2. Which falsifiable hypothesis does it test?
3. Which evidence establishes the underlying gap or bottleneck?
4. Does the scientific proposition survive removal of the implementation name?

Park the proposal when an upstream answer is missing. Return to that layer without discarding the idea.

## Freeze scope

Record the research object, unit, population, context, exposure or intervention family, outcomes, time horizon, boundaries, and maximum claim. Changing any of these after freeze creates a new brief version and preserves the earlier decision record.

Keep these judgments distinct:

- the problem exists;
- a hypothesis is plausible;
- an experiment is feasible;
- a candidate mechanism is effective;
- a practical benefit exists; and
- the result transfers beyond the studied population.
