# Primary Evidence Review

Review each included source at the level needed by the claim it supports.

## Evidence record

Record:

- stable source ID, title, authors or owner, version, date, and durable URL or identifier;
- source type and primary-owner status;
- verification date and access level;
- research object, population, unit, sample, denominator, and setting;
- intervention, comparison, method, instruments, and analysis;
- directly reported result with uncertainty and conditions;
- author-stated limitations, corrections, and retraction status;
- reproducibility artifacts and their versions;
- reviewer inference, alternative explanation, and transfer limit; and
- claims in the brief that use the record.

## Evidence labels

- `DIRECT`: Observed in primary data or directly reported by the owning source.
- `SOURCE_CLAIM`: Explicitly claimed by the source but not independently verified here.
- `INFERENCE`: Derived from multiple records; list the inference steps.
- `UNKNOWN`: The reviewed material cannot establish the proposition.

State unreported content as unreported. State an absent finding only when the source's method and results establish absence at a declared sensitivity. Qualify a research-status claim to the search coverage and cutoff.

## Evidence card

For each coherent finding, write:

1. one complete judgment;
2. the object and population studied;
3. the method and comparison;
4. the directly relevant result;
5. the denominator, uncertainty, and conditions;
6. the narrow conclusion supported; and
7. what remains unresolved.

Place quantities next to their populations and denominators. Keep cross-source synthesis separate from any single source's claims.

## Synthesis discipline

Compare records only after aligning object, unit, population, denominator, outcome definition, measurement window, and baseline. Treat multiple papers, datasets, or reviews derived from the same underlying evidence as one dependency cluster when judging independence.

Retain conflicting results. Explain whether they reflect sampling uncertainty, population shift, measurement differences, analytic choices, source dependence, or a genuinely unresolved conflict.
